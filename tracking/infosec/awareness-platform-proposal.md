# Security Awareness Platform — proposal awaiting Mancom decision

Status: **For presentation to Mancom** · Raised: July 2026

## Why this is on the table

The coop's security awareness training and phishing testing ran for 12 months on
**Wizer**, and that subscription has now ended. It was not renewed straight away, because
the department wanted to look at what is on offer before committing again.

**This is a change of supplier, not a renewal.** That matters for how it is presented:
the Mancom will fairly ask what the coop is gaining by moving from Wizer to KnowBe4, and
the report has to answer that rather than assume it.

The result is that **from July 2026 the coop has no security awareness training and no
phishing testing running.** Staff are not being trained and are not being tested. That
gap stays open until a decision is made.

## What is being proposed

A subscription to **KnowBe4**, a security awareness training and simulated phishing
platform, supplied through **MASS-SPECC** (the cooperative development centre BUGEMCO
already works with).

In plain terms, it does two things:

1. **Trains staff** — short lessons in a web browser that staff work through at their
   own pace, teaching them how to spot scam emails, fake websites and phone tricks.
2. **Tests staff** — sends harmless fake scam emails to see who would fall for a real
   one, so training can be aimed where it is actually needed.

## The problem with the proposal as it stands

**The proposal on file is dated 5 September 2024, and it states that the prices quoted
are valid for 7 days and are subject to change.**

That means the figures above are almost two years old and are no longer a live quote.
They are useful for showing the Mancom the shape and rough size of the commitment, but
a fresh quotation has to be requested before anyone signs anything or approves a budget
figure.

The report must say this plainly. Presenting a two-year-old price as the current price
would put a wrong number in front of the committee.

## When this would actually start

**Not this year.** Confirmed by the owner in the July 2026 intake: if the Mancom approves
it, the subscription would begin **in 2027, funded from next year's annual budget.**

This is the single most important fact about the proposal and it changes what the report
must say.

- The August presentation is **not** a request to buy something now. It is a request to
  evaluate and to place the item in next year's budget.
- **The gap is not a few weeks. It runs to the end of 2026 at least.** From July 2026
  onwards the coop has no security awareness training and no phishing testing, and on the
  current plan it will not have any until 2027.
- The BSC phishing target therefore **cannot be measured for the rest of 2026.** Not
  missed — not measurable.

## What Wizer cost, and how the two compare

Supplied by the owner in the August 2026 review.

| | Wizer (what the coop had) | KnowBe4 (proposed) |
|---|---|---|
| People covered | 29 employees | 100 accounts |
| Length | 1 year | 3 years |
| **Total cost** | **Php 50,000.00** | **Php 1,000,000.00** |
| Cost per year | Php 50,000.00 | Php 333,333.33 |
| Per person per year | Php 1,724.14 | Php 3,333.33 |
| Per person per month | Php 143.68 | Php 277.78 |

Only the two totals and the two headcounts come from a source. Everything else in
that table is arithmetic on them.

**Per person, KnowBe4 is about 1.9 times Wizer** — roughly 93% more per person per
year. That is the like-for-like number.

**The annual bill rises further than that** because the coverage rises too, from 29
people to 100: Php 50,000 a year becomes Php 333,333 a year, about **6.7 times** the
annual spend. Most of that increase buys people who were not covered before, not a
dearer product. Presenting the 6.7× on its own would be misleading, and so would
presenting the 1.9× on its own.

**Two things stop this being a clean comparison, and both need settling before the
figures decide anything:**

1. The KnowBe4 quote is for **100 accounts**. If the coop needs 29, or 60, it must be
   re-quoted for the real number before the totals mean anything.
2. It is a **three-year commitment against a one-year one**. That is a different kind
   of decision even at the same price.

*Still to confirm:* whether the Php 50,000 for Wizer included VAT. The KnowBe4 figure
states 12% VAT is included; if Wizer's does not, the gap is smaller than it looks.

## The honest position on comparison

**Wizer and KnowBe4 have not been properly compared yet.** Confirmed by the owner.

The main factor in KnowBe4's favour so far is that it comes through **MASS-SPECC**, the
federation BUGEMCO belongs to — a body the coop already deals with, rather than an
unknown supplier.

That is a real advantage and worth stating. It is not, on its own, a comparison. The
report must present this as a proposal under evaluation, not as a settled recommendation,
because a committee that is told the options were weighed will ask how.

## What the Mancom is being asked for

Two things, and they are different:

1. **For August:** evaluate the proposal, and decide whether it goes into the 2027 annual
   budget.
2. **Separately, and more urgently:** decide what happens to security awareness in the
   meantime. On the current plan there will be none for the rest of 2026.

## A gap worth naming

If the platform cannot start until 2027, the months between are still worth something.
Awareness sessions run in-house cost nothing but time, and the department has run them
before — three are on record for 2026 already. That is a decision for the owner and the
Mancom, not an assumption to build into the report, but leaving five months with nothing
at all should be a choice somebody makes rather than something that just happens.

## Open questions for the owner

- **Why move from Wizer to KnowBe4?** This is the question the Mancom will ask first, and
  the answer is not yet on record. What did Wizer not do, or what does KnowBe4 do better?
- Was Wizer's renewal price compared against this one?
- How many staff would actually need covering now — the proposal is priced for 100.
- Has a fresh, current quotation been requested from MASS-SPECC?
- Are any other suppliers being compared, or is this the only offer?

## Redaction note — read before publishing

The report goes on a public website. The source document is a commercial proposal and
contains things that should not be published:

- The **names and job titles** of the coop's Chairperson and Chief Executive Officer,
  and of the supplier's Chief Executive Officer
- The supplier's **contact email address**
- The coop's **street address**
- The **exact peso figures** of a live commercial negotiation

Naming the platform and describing what it does is fine. Publishing a supplier's pricing
and the officers' names is not. The published version should say that a proposal is
under evaluation and describe what it offers, and keep the peso figures and the names
for the internal version shown in the meeting.
