### Note — digital agreements: drafted is not the same as issued

**All five digital agreements were drafted on 20 August 2026**, which is the whole of the
target's count in a single month. They are listed in `../infosec/policies-guidelines.md`.

**But none of them has been given to a member.** Each carries conditions that bar it from
being issued, and the department's own documentation is explicit that a draft existing on
paper does not lift that bar.

**Decided in the August intake, reversed on 16 September 2026.**

The first decision was that *standardised* meant written to a common standard, which made
the target **5 of 5, met**. The owner reopened it himself: *"why do we count the 5 digital
agreements as good, it's not even presented and approved by the Mancom yet."*

**The actual is now recorded as a dash, with five drafted noted beside it.** Nothing is
claimed against the target until the agreements have been presented and approved.

The reasoning, for whoever reads this next: a figure shown as met invites the question "so
they are in use?", and the answer is no. The qualifier "none issued yet" was carrying more
weight than a qualifier can carry on a projected slide. **Five drafts in one month is a
real month's work and the report still says so** — it is reported under Information
Security as work delivered, rather than on the scorecard as a target reached.

**What would make it 5 of 5:** presented to Mancom, approved, and issued. The presentation
is the next step and it is on the list of things the committee can clear.

### Note — the trainings attended figure rests on the owner's word alone

Through July 2026 this target sat at **0 of 4**, and the July intake recorded that the
owner had attended sessions which were never written down.

**In the August 2026 intake the owner stated that the trainings are completed**, and the
actual is recorded as **4 of 4** on that basis. The same statement is taken to cover
Deliverable #2, recorded as **1 of 1**.

**No list of sessions exists.** Not a name, a date, a provider or a certificate. If Mancom
asks "which trainings?", there is nothing on file to answer with. The figure is the
owner's own confirmation and the report should not dress it up as anything more.

**Ask for the list next month.** Four named sessions would turn this from a claim into a
result, and it is the only target on the scorecard resting on nothing but a statement.

### Note — how the information drives count was settled

March and May each list an "Information Drive". The source material files both under
**events supported**, which is not the same as an event held, and it does not say which
these actually were.

**The owner's decision, given in the July 2026 intake: both count regardless.** That put
the actual at **2 of 5**.

**August 2026: two members' sessions were conducted, and the owner's decision is that each
one counts as a separate information drive.** That takes the actual to **4 of 5** — one
short of the year's target, with four months still to run.

Both decisions are recorded as the owner's judgement rather than as something the source
documents establish, so that anyone reading later knows where the figure came from. No
attendance figures exist for the August sessions; the count is of sessions held.

### Note — the two 3.69 figures

The scorecard sets the **target** for incident response time at 3.69. The Network and
System Administration July 2026 report gives an **actual** average response time of 3.69
hours. The same number appearing as both the target and the result is very unlikely to be
a coincidence.

The likely explanation is that the 3.69 in the scorecard was copied from this same
measurement at some earlier point, which would mean the two are one number travelling
between documents rather than a result that happens to land exactly on target.

**Confirm with the owner before either figure is reported**, and specifically:
- Is "incident response time" the same measurement as the support team's "average
  response time", or two different things?
- If they are the same, what was the target actually meant to be?

Until that is settled, the actual column stays blank. Reporting "3.69 against a target of
3.69 — exactly on target" would be a very tidy claim built on an unchecked assumption.

## Note on the phishing figure — needs confirming

The BSC calls for a **failure rate of 1% or less** — that is, how many staff click
the fake test email.

Another document on record measures the same thing the other way round, as a
**pass rate**, where a high number is good. The two cannot both be failure rates:
one of them would mean almost everyone clicked.

(The figures in that other document are personal performance data and are
deliberately not repeated here. This site is public.)

Until the owner confirms which is meant, this file records **failure rate: lower
is better**, and every report states the unit in plain words rather than a bare
percentage — the form to use is "*N* of *M* staff clicked the test email (*X*%)",
so nobody has to guess which direction the number runs.

## Where the "Actual so far" figures come from

The planning spreadsheet holds targets only — its monthly columns were left empty,
so it records no actual results for 2026. Every dash above is therefore a genuine
"nothing recorded yet", not a measured result of zero. The dashes are filled in
month by month from what the owner reports.

The one exception is **Security breaches**, which shows a real 0. That figure is
not from the planning spreadsheet: it is corroborated by Deliverable #9 in
`deliverables-2026.md`, which records major security breaches as 0 against a
target of 0.
