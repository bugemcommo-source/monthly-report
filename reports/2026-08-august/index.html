Two things to fix, neither costs anything

  • All 5,041 are recorded as visitors and none as a member, so we cannot say how much of the queue is our own members
  • 60 tickets were never closed in the system — the longest shows five and a half days at the counter

Both are how the system is being used, not faults in it.

This is the first hard service data the coop has ever had, and it comes from three exports the Queuing System produced. This is the whole of August — nineteen working days, the third to the twenty-eighth. The two working days with nothing in them are the twenty-first and the thirty-first, Ninoy Aquino Day and National Heroes Day, so there is no gap to explain.

Five thousand and forty-one tickets were issued, an average of two hundred and sixty-five a day, and four thousand nine hundred and thirty-nine of them ended in a completed transaction. A hundred and two did not, which is two per cent, so please take the big number as tickets issued rather than as people served — they are not the same figure and I would rather say so than let it pass.

The headline I want you to take is the median, five point six minutes: half of everyone waited that or less. The average is twelve point four, but a small number of very long waits drag it up and it describes almost nobody, which is why it is not the number on the wall. Sixty-six in every hundred were served within ten minutes and eighty-nine within thirty.

Cash Transaction takes forty point nine per cent of everyone and moves them fastest, at three point five minutes; Accounts Services takes one visitor in sixteen and keeps them waiting sixteen point four. Insurance is not in that table on purpose — sixteen tickets in nineteen days is too few to rank a counter on, and for the record its median wait was nine point eight minutes.

The finding I would act on is late morning. Nine o'clock takes the most people of any hour, eight hundred and thirty-seven, and moves them in eleven minutes. Eleven o'clock takes five hundred and forty-two, two thirds as many, and they wait nearly nineteen — the worst hour of the day. The queue does not thin before lunch; the counters do, an hour earlier than any of us would have guessed. That is a staffing question, not a systems one.

Worth saying plainly: the partial export we had first pointed at noon, and the full month moves it to eleven. That is exactly why it was worth waiting for the whole month before asking you to act on it.

Last, two things we can fix for nothing: every single person is being tagged as a visitor and nobody as a member, so we cannot tell you how much of our own queue is our own membership; and sixty tickets were never closed in the system, one of them showing five and a half days at the counter, which is obviously somebody forgetting to close it rather than a real service time. Those sixty are left out of every average on this slide.
